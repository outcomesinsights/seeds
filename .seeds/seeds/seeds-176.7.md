@aguynamedryan coming around to a core framing for the post (stream-of-consciousness, verbatim-ish):

seeds, by its design, brings @aguynamedryan THROUGH the design process. Not linear, not a baked-in workflow — but the core move of the seeds workflow does the dragging: "I've got this idea I want to do stuff with. What do you think we need to figure out about it before AI is comfortable implementing it?" Coming at a feature from that angle "drags me through figuring out some of the details" — without getting "mired in a giant spec document."

VS POLL: Poll frames planning as the thing to skip. But Poll was really mitigating the EXPENSIVE part of software development — sitting down and actually writing code. "Thinking through a problem has always been part of software development" — working out what the problem is, and "what the REAL problem is we're trying to solve." (Cf. the countless articles about figuring out the real problem a client or customer is having before deciding how to build software to fix it.) AI made CODE cheap; it did not make THINKING cheap. So planning isn't obsolete — its cadence changes (cf. the Ball->Poll bridge in seeds-176.1), but the design/discovery work remains.

THE "ARCHITECT" SHIFT: @aguynamedryan surrendered code-writing to GenAI — something he used to enjoy — to build more, faster. In effect he got "promoted to architect," and he wanted a tool to help him in that role. seeds is that tool. (Honest hedge, verbatim-ish: "maybe that's not the right approach in an AI era, but it's the approach that works for me to generate what I believe to be the most effective systems.")

This is the affirmative core of the journey thesis: the journey is the design/discovery work, and that work is where @aguynamedryan's value now lives. The destination (the code/contract) is cheap; the journey to deciding what it should be is the craft.
