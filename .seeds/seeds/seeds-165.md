The sharpest critique in the AI-recording literature (a16z "Everything Is Recorded Now"; White & Case on governance) is the observer effect: once people assume everything is recorded, the messy half-formed thinking that produces good decisions migrates to the unrecorded hallway — "you capture more context, but a thinner, more performed version of it."

@aguynamedryan's verdict (2026-06-15): this mostly is NOT his environment, and that is worth recording. For the work he does there are **no hallways** — conversations are either directly with an AI or in a Zoom meeting, all transcribed; he and his boss *intentionally* sought this out and *want* the decisions recorded. Everything is on the record by design.

He concedes the effect is real, though, and benign in his case: knowing it is captured *does* make him more deliberate — he will call out "note this" or flag a part of the conversation as important, which is really small direction to a future agent about when something matters. He questions whether "performative" is even the right worry: "isn't talking at work performative? shouldn't people have decorum, stick to the topic?" He lands on **tactfully candid** — and on that footing seeds works perfectly for him as-is.

Positioning upside worth saying out loud: seeds is the *antidote* to ambient recording — the place candid deliberation is *conducted intentionally and in the loop*, not ambiently surveilled.
