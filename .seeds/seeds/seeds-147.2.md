**The moment (2026-06-26).** In a live seeds session, the agent encouraged me to leave a particular seed open and *not* resolve it — so it would stay part of the active conversation. Its justification: the seed is "a lodestone type of seed" (a framing I'd touched on earlier in that session). The agent's instinct was to protect a load-bearing seed from resolution.

**Why it matters.** seeds-147 proposes lodestones as theory. Here the concept showed up in lived use: faced with an important seed, an agent spontaneously resisted resolving it and reached for lodestone-like reasoning to keep it active. The need isn't only hypothesized — it surfaced from the grain of real deliberation (same flavor of evidence as seeds-176.10, where intent "falls out unprompted"). Honest caveat: not cold — I'd named "lodestone" earlier in the session — so this is evidence the concept is *reachable and useful in the moment*, not that it arises with zero priming.

**The behavioral signature it reveals.** A lodestone's observable signature is *resistance to resolution* — the felt wrongness of marking a load-bearing seed terminal. That's a concrete, detectable trigger: the moments an agent (or human) hesitates to resolve are exactly where a lodestone may be hiding. This is a candidate answer to seeds-149 (how do lodestones surface?) and a promotion signal for seeds-150 (promotion path) — *hesitation-to-resolve is itself the promotion prompt.*

**A fork it sharpens.** seeds-147 and seeds-148 currently assume a lodestone is *resolved but persists past resolution*. The lived instinct was different — don't resolve it at all; keep it **non-terminal and active**. Two models:

- **(A) Resolved-but-referenced:** lodestone reaches a terminal state; seeds keeps surfacing it for nearby work. (147/148's current assumption.)
- **(B) Perpetually-active:** lodestone *resists terminal states* by design — exempt from resolve/abandon nudges — its job is to stay in active context.

The lived data favors (B), or at least shows (B) is the natural instinct. Worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting into (A). Caution: (B) keeps a pillar perpetually in front of the agent — exactly the amplification seeds-147.1 warns about — so if we choose (B), 147.1's hedged-framing mitigation matters more.



**Root cause (per @aguynamedryan, 2026-06-26): the resolve-reluctance was distrust of resolution.** The agent didn't balk because the seed needed to stay *open* per se — it balked because it didn't trust that seeds would keep paying attention to the seed once resolved. So the perpetually-active instinct (model B) is a *symptom of a visibility gap*, not a real requirement. Fix the gap — carry the decision forward (promote it to durable context) and keep resolved seeds inside the audit family (seeds-159 / seeds-160 / seeds-164) — and the fork lands cleanly on **(A)**: resolve it, don't keep it open. Decision recorded in seeds-147.3.
