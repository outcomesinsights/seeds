Storey's Triple Debt Model (ACM Queue / arXiv, out of the Thoughtworks retreat, Feb 2026) gives a peer-reviewed name to seeds' reason to exist: *intent debt* = "the absence of externalized rationale that developers and AI agents need to work safely with code," living in artifacts. The gap analysis proposed adopting that vocabulary as seeds' external positioning.

@aguynamedryan's decision (2026-06-15): **declined to adopt whole hog**, recorded as a boundary. "Intent is the wrong unit." The intent-debt framing captures what you *decided you want* and *why* — but not **what was considered and discarded along the way**, which to him is the important part: so you do not re-litigate a dismissed idea as if it were new, and so you remember *why* you dismissed it. "Intent is still further to the right" — more downstream, more settled — than what seeds captures. seeds captures the *journey that produces* intent, not the intent.

The better, inverse framing is captured separately (see the "seeds is upstream of intent" idea). Opting *not* to adopt the vocabulary is itself a design decision worth recording: "if I have opted not to adopt intent debt whole hog, then I think it's important that we record that."
