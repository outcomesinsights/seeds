Both, in that order. The outside-in reviewer works in two phases: first it gets ONLY the problem statement and proposes independently; then it is shown our solution and critiques it directly. Sequencing preserves what makes divergence meaningful — its proposal exists before it has seen ours, so it cannot be a graded reaction to ours — while still buying the direct critique that a problem-only review never produces.

Cost: two round trips for that reviewer, and a commitment effect. Having proposed, it may defend its own proposal against ours rather than assess ours on its merits. It must be told explicitly that phase one is not a position to defend and that agreeing with us in phase two is a valid outcome.
